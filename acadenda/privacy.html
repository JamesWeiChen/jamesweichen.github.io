Privacy Policy

Applies to all apps published by James Chen on the App Store. Last updated: 17 September 2026

These apps do not collect, transmit, or share any personal data. There are no accounts, no sign-in, no analytics, no advertising, and no tracking of any kind. No data is sent to the developer.

What stays on your device

Everything you create or import stays on the device you created it on. That includes the files you import, anything you write or draw, and small preference values such as settings and purchase status. Where a value needs to survive reinstalling an app, it is stored in the device keychain. None of it leaves your device.

No app listed here includes an analytics, attribution, or crash-reporting SDK.

Purchases

Any in-app purchases are handled entirely by Apple's App Store and StoreKit. The apps never see your payment information. Data related to a transaction is processed by Apple under Apple's Privacy Policy.

Network access

There is no server on our side. No app listed here sends your content to the developer, because there is nowhere for it to go. Network activity is limited to:

  • requests the operating system makes on an app's behalf — for example, contacting the App Store to present or restore an in-app purchase;
  • a direct, local connection between two of your own devices;
  • an optional lookup of a public catalogue that you switch on and trigger yourself.

Where an app does any of the last two, it is spelled out under that app below — including exactly what is sent.

External displays and AirPlay

When an app shows content on a projector, TV, or other external display over HDMI or AirPlay, that is a local display connection. Nothing is uploaded.

Children's privacy

These apps collect no data from anyone, including children.

App-specific details

Beamer Notes

PDF decks you import are copied into the app's private storage, and your Apple Pencil annotations are saved locally alongside them. To count how many distinct PDFs the free tier has been used for, the app stores a list of content hashes of those PDFs — not the files, their names, or their contents — together with your purchase status, in the device keychain. These values never leave your device.

Beamer Notes can also be controlled from an iPhone acting as a remote for a nearby iPad. When you use that, the two devices connect directly to each other over Apple's MultipeerConnectivity (local network and/or Bluetooth) and exchange only what the remote needs to display: images of the current and next slide, the speaker-notes text of the current slide, the timer value, and page or laser-pointer commands. This connection is between your own two devices only. There is no server, no relay, and no account; nothing is stored off your devices, and the connection exists only while you are using the remote.

Acadenda

Conference programmes you import, the talks and speakers in them, and your notes are stored on the device. Nothing is uploaded.

Reading programmes. Turning a programme into structured talks is done by Apple's on-device language model. The text never leaves your device and is not sent to us or to any AI service.

Voice notes. If you record a voice note, the audio is saved on the device and transcribed by Apple's on-device speech recognition. Both the recording and the transcript stay on the device.

Optional speaker lookup. The app can look a speaker up in OpenAlex, a free public catalogue of scholarly work, to show where they work. This is off unless you turn it on, and runs only when you tap to search. The only thing sent is the speaker's name as printed in the programme — no identifier of you, your device, or anything else in the app. There is no account and no API key. What OpenAlex does with a query it receives is governed by its own privacy practices.

Links out. Menu items that search for a speaker's papers on Google Scholar or Semantic Scholar open your browser at those sites with the name in the search field. The app itself does not contact them; following such a link is a visit you make to that site, under its own privacy practices.

Tips. If you leave a tip, it is an ordinary App Store purchase handled by Apple, as described above. Tips unlock nothing and are not required to use the app.
